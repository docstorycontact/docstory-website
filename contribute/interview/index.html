Your answers

Write the way you’d talk to a friend who just got an interview invite to your school.

General guidance
  • Show, don’t tell. Instead of saying something is good or bad, describe a moment that shows it.
  • Use the questions as a starting point. Many have several parts. You don’t have to answer every part; they’re there to spark ideas and point you in directions to expand on.
  • Every good answer has a few specific details. A course, a site, a person, a number, a moment: they’re what applicants can’t find anywhere else.
  • Include the downsides. Where your school has room to improve is some of the most useful information for applicants. You don’t need to trash your program; an honest, balanced take is best.
  • Leave out anything that could identify a patient.
We publish interviews of similar depth to the ones already on DocStory. If some answers are thin, we’ll ask you to expand them before publishing.
