What a good answer looks like

Real answers, as published on DocStory. No essays needed: a few honest, specific details are what applicants can’t find anywhere else.

Class culture

124 words

How would you describe the student culture at your school?

My medical school cohort is very small (34 students), which has a big impact on the culture. We also have dedicated "social chairs" which help organize and plan events to help students get involved and have fun. I also think our cohort has a very collaborative and supportive culture. We frequently pick each other up when students have car troubles, help drive each other to the airports, go on vacations together, etc. Although it sounds trite, it does feel like a family in many regards! There are some cons of it being small/family-like; there is more gossip and it is harder to keep as much privacy as in larger classes, so you definitely take the good with the bad in regards to class size.

C
Caleb
MS3 · Texas A&M College of Medicine (EnMed)

Big picture

Excerpt, 68 words

What would you consider strengths of your medical school?

…The real strength of UCLA lies in the excellent clinical training across a variety of sites. Being exposed to strong residents and faculty who will delegate significant responsibility to you as a medical student is the best way to maximize your learning during third year. If I was applying to medical school again, I would focus intensely on the amount of responsibility given to medical students as MS3s.

TE
Tim Ellis-Caleo
MS4 · David Geffen School of Medicine at UCLA

Service

86 words

What organizations or activities are you involved in outside of your regular classwork?

At UCSD, we have a Free Clinic so awesome that almost every single student is able to participate in it. I manage the neurology and psychiatry free clinics with some of my fellow students, and it is by far the most fulfilling thing I have done in medical school. The patients are incredible teachers, and it is a genuine honor to be able to learn from them. If you come to UCSD’s Second Look, I really recommend touring the Free Clinic and learning more about it.

N
Neela
MS1 · UC San Diego School of Medicine

What we’ll ask you

Choose 8–10 of these 44 questions, or add one of your own. When you start, we suggest a set based on your year and the topics you care about. Each answer needs at least 40 words; the examples above run 68 to 124.

Big picture · 6 questionsexpand_more
  1. What do you think is a shortcoming of your school or an area that could be improved? Be specific.
  2. Who thrives here, and who would be happier somewhere else?
  3. Why did you choose this school over your other options, and would you make the same choice today?
  4. Have there been moments or experiences you've had that made you glad you chose this medical school/reaffirmed your choice? Have there been some moments that made you question it?
  5. What has been different from what you expected based on interview day or the school's materials, for better or worse?
  6. What is one thing about this school that deserves more credit than it gets?
Pre-clinical · 8 questionsexpand_more
  1. Walk us through a typical week in the pre-clinical curriculum. Roughly how much is lecture, small group, lab, and self-study?
  2. How are the preclinical years graded (pass/fail, tiers, internal ranking, etc.)? Do you think this has a major impact on the student culture at your school?
  3. Is lecture attendance required? What do most of your classmates actually do (attend, stream, study from outside resources, etc.)?
  4. What was the adjustment to the first semester like, and what would you tell an incoming student to do differently? Did your school do anything specific to help you through the first semester and where did you feel on your own?
  5. Which course, block, or professor has been the best part of the curriculum, and why?
  6. Which part of the curriculum do you think has room for improvement, and what specifically is not working?
  7. What experiences have you had before clerkships (preceptorships, student-run clinics, standardized patients, simulation) to give you at least some exposure to clinical work prior to third year? What experiences provided by your medical school do you think are most helpful in making this transition successful?
  8. How is clinical skills teaching organized, and how confident do you feel taking a history and doing a physical exam right now?
Clinical · 8 questionsexpand_more
  1. Briefly describe the main hospitals and clinics you work at and what sort of patient population you see at each. Has one of these clinical sites or clinics been particularly rewarding or influential for you? We want to know about experiences that might be unique to your medical school. For 3rd and 4th years
  2. Walk us through a recent day on your current or most recent rotation. For 3rd and 4th years
  3. During clinical rotations, were you happy with how directly involved you were in patient care and your value on the team? What did you think about the balance between autonomy and guidance/teaching during your rotations? This is going to differ for every rotation and team you are a part of, so feel free to give examples you feel are representative. For 3rd and 4th years
  4. Which rotation was your strongest and which was your weakest, and what made the difference? For 3rd and 4th years
  5. Have there been particular challenges with the logistics of clinical rotations that you wish were different? For example, distance between sites, number of sites to be covered, challenge getting select rotations you want, etc. For 3rd and 4th years
  6. How would you describe the culture of feedback during rotations? Were you happy with how/when feedback was given and did you find that it was generally constructive feedback? For 3rd and 4th years
  7. Looking back, how well did the pre-clinical years prepare you for the wards? Did you feel by the time clerkships came about, you had an adequate foundation of knowledge to start building clinical skills? Do you feel that foundation of knowledge was a result of structured course work or self-study? For 3rd and 4th years
  8. While residents, attendings, and fellows you work with are going to differ from rotation to rotation, can you describe an experience that you think is representative of the housestaff culture at the clinical sites you are at? For 3rd and 4th years
Research · 4 questionsexpand_more
  1. Was it challenging to find a research mentor and do you think a medical student that arrives with no research experience would be able to find a research mentor if they desired? Describe how you or a classmate actually got connected with one.
  2. Beyond finding a mentor, you also need the time to be able to conduct research projects you are a part of. Is it feasible for medical students to participate in research during pre-clinical and clinical years? Does the program do something to facilitate this participation?
  3. What research are you a part of and what is your specific role in it?
  4. Have you presented or published your work? What support did the school give you, such as funding, conference travel, or help with abstracts? For 2nd–4th years
Global health · 4 questionsexpand_more
  1. Have you participated in global health opportunities? If so, describe what the opportunity was and how it was valuable as a personal experience and as a part of your medical education.
  2. For an applicant who wants a career in global health, what makes this school a good or a poor choice?
  3. Are there chances to work with underserved populations? If so, describe the population you work with, the experience itself, and how it contributes to your medical training.
  4. If you were interested in global health but haven't done anything abroad, what got in the way: cost, timing, or how the curriculum is set up?
Advocacy · 4 questionsexpand_more
  1. What community service or outreach are you involved in? What does the work look like and how has it been meaningful to you? If you are not involved in any, is there work that other medical students are doing at your school that you think is particularly meaningful?
  2. If you've done a track or elective in equity, advocacy, or policy, what did it involve, and was it worth the time?
  3. Have students organized around an issue on campus or in the community? What happened?
  4. Does the community within which your medical school is located have a significant impact on the clinical work that you are doing? Is the patient population unique in a way that gives medical students significantly more exposure to a particular type of medicine that may not be the case at other schools?
Lifestyle · 5 questionsexpand_more
  1. How would you describe your class: collaborative, competitive, diverse, close? Give an example that shows it.
  2. What area do you live in? Do you think this area makes it easy to do things to decompress when you have down time? Give examples of things you or other students might do in the area for fun.
  3. How responsive is the administration to student feedback? Give an example of something that is currently being discussed, was recently changed, or did not change after students spoke up.
  4. When school got hard, what helped you cope, and did the school's own support make a difference?
  5. Roughly what does a month cost you here (rent, food, getting around), and how far does the financial aid budget really stretch?
Boards, advising & the Match · 5 questionsexpand_more
  1. How well did the curriculum prepare you for Step 1 or Level 1? To expand, did you feel the designed curriculum was a major contributor to your success on the exams, or was this mainly a result of dedicated study on your own? How much dedicated study time did you get, and did you feel it was enough? For 2nd–4th years
  2. How does specialty and career advising work? Who advised you, and how useful was it?
  3. Broadly speaking, how do you feel about the success of students at your medical school in the match? While there is always going to be variation in the match rate across specialties, did you feel there were specialties that were difficult to match into from your medical school? Or residency programs/institutions that were difficult to match to from your medical school?
  4. Are you happy with the extent to which your school supported you in your efforts to match at the program of your choice (in terms of letters of recommendation, phone calls to program leadership, providing general guidance, etc.)? For 4th years
  5. Are there specialties at your school that are particularly strong and therefore typically garner a lot of interest amongst students?

Questions

Can I stay anonymous?expand_more

Yes. You choose how you’re credited: full name, first name only, or anonymous. We still need your name and school email privately to verify you and pay you, but neither is ever published.

Why do you need my .edu email?expand_more

It’s how we confirm every interview comes from a real student at that school. Before anything is published, we email that address and ask you to reply. We only email you from docstory.contact@gmail.com, and we never ask for passwords or bank details.

Will my answers be edited?expand_more

Lightly, for formatting, length, and clarity. We don’t change what you meant. You’ll see the edited version in our verification email and nothing goes live until you approve it.

When and how do I get paid?expand_more

You choose how you’re paid when you reply to the verification email we send your school (.edu) address: just tell us whether you’d like PayPal, Venmo, or an Amazon.com eGift card, and where to send it: your PayPal or Venmo username or email, or the email address for the gift card. Payment goes out within 7 days of your interview going live. We never ask for bank details.

My school already has 5 interviews. Can I still contribute?expand_more

Yes, and we’d love more perspectives, but we aren’t paying for new interviews from schools with 5 or more published right now. The form tells you as soon as you pick your school.

Can I use ChatGPT or other AI tools?expand_more

Not to write your answers. Applicants come to DocStory for real students’ own words, so AI-written answers aren’t accepted or paid. Spelling and grammar tools are fine, and so is drafting somewhere else and pasting it in. To help us review, the form notes how much of each answer was typed versus pasted and roughly how long it took.

Can readers contact me?expand_more

Only if you opt in. When you submit, you can choose to receive follow-up questions from readers until you graduate. Questions come through DocStory: we forward each one to your school email or a personal email you choose, and your address is never shown on the site. You decide whether to answer, either directly or through us to stay anonymous, and you can opt out anytime.

Do I have to finish in one sitting?expand_more

No. Your draft saves automatically in your browser, so you can close the page and pick up where you left off on the same device.

Is there a quality bar?expand_more

Yes. Whether an interview is ready to publish is at our discretion, and the bar is roughly the caliber of the typical interview already on DocStory: specific, first-hand answers with real detail. We make every effort to accept and pay for every good-faith submission, so if something falls short we’ll usually ask you to expand a few answers rather than turn it down.

What if my interview isn’t accepted?expand_more

We’ll tell you why. Most often it’s an unverifiable email, answers that are too thin, or details that could identify a patient. We’ll usually ask you to revise first; if it still doesn’t meet the bar after that, we may decline it, and declined interviews aren’t paid.

Can I take my interview down later?expand_more

Yes. Email us from your school address and we’ll remove it or switch it to anonymous. See the Contributor Terms for details.
